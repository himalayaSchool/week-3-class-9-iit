02 / Materials

Components Used

1
Arduino UNO

Reads sensor data and controls the driver.

2
HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor

Measures distance to objects using sound pulses.

3
L298N motor driver

Controls motor direction and current supply.

4
Two DC geared motors

Turn the wheels to move the robot.

5
Chassis and wheels

Mechanical frame and drive system.

6
Battery pack and wires

Provides power and electrical connections.

03 / Pin map

Connection Guide

Component pinConnect to
HC-SR04 VCCArduino 5V
HC-SR04 TRIGArduino D9
HC-SR04 ECHOArduino D10
HC-SR04 GNDArduino GND
L298N IN1 / IN2Arduino D5 / D6
L298N IN3 / IN4Arduino D7 / D8
L298N ENA / ENBKeep enable jumpers fitted for simple full-speed control
L298N OUT1 / OUT2One DC motor
L298N OUT3 / OUT4Other DC motor
Motor supply + / GNDBattery + / battery − at driver power terminals
Arduino GNDCommon ground with L298N GND
Power safety: Never connect motors directly to Arduino pins. Use a battery matched to the motor and driver ratings, check polarity and measure the pack voltage. Connect Arduino GND and driver GND together. While testing, powering the Arduino by USB is a simple option. Use the driver’s 5V output only after checking the exact board’s regulator/jumper instructions.
04 / Build

Procedure

1

Assemble the chassisSecure the two geared motors, wheels and support wheel to the frame.

2

Mount the sensorAttach the HC-SR04 at the front, facing forward, with its transducers unobstructed.

3

Wire the circuitConnect sensor, Arduino, motor driver, motors and battery using the pin map above.

4

Upload the programLoad code that measures distance and sends movement commands to the motor driver.

5

Check the motorsLift the wheels clear of the floor, switch on and verify both motors respond correctly.

6

Test obstacle avoidanceSet a safe detection threshold (for example, about 20 cm), test in an open space and tune the turning time.
